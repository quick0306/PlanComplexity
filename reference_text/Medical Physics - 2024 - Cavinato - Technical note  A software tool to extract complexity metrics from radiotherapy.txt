Abstract
Background: Complexity metrics are mathematical quantities designed to
quantify aspects of radiotherapy treatment plans that may affect both their deliv-
erability and dosimetric accuracy . Despite numerous studies investigating their
utility , there remains a notable absence of shared tools for their extraction.
Purpose: This study introduces UCoMX (Universal Complexity Metrics Extrac-
tor), a software package designed for the extraction of complexity metrics from
the DICOM-RT plan ﬁles of radiotherapy treatments.
Methods: UCoMX is developed around two extraction engines: VCoMX (VMAT
Complexity Metrics Extractor) for VMAT/IMRT plans, and TCoMX (T omotherapy
Complexity Metrics Extractor) tailored for Helical T omotherapy plans. The soft-
ware,built using Matlab,is freely available in both Matlab-based and stand-alone
versions. More than 90 complexity metrics, drawn from relevant literature, are
implemented in the package: 43 for VMAT/IMRT and 51 for Helical T omotherapy .
Results: The package is designed to read DICOM-RT plan ﬁles generated by
most commercially available T reatment Planning Systems (TPSs), across var-
ious treatment units. A reference dataset containing VMAT , IMRT , and Helical
T omotherapy plans is provided to serve as a reference for comparing UCoMX
with other in-house systems available at other centers.
Conclusion: UCoMX offers a straightforward solution for extracting complexity
metrics from radiotherapy plans. Its versatility is enhanced through different ver-
sions, including Matlab-based and stand-alone, and its compatibility with a wide
range of commercially available TPSs and treatment units. UCoMX presents a
free, user-friendly tool empowering researchers to compute the complexity of
treatment plans efﬁciently .

In this context, complexity metrics are mathematical indi-

apy plan properties that aim to quantify such aspects. 5
Over the last 20 years, several studies have explored
the ﬁeld of plan complexity , either by proposing new
ad hoc complexity indicators designed to quantify a
variety of plan characteristics, 5,6 or by investigating
their relationship with patient-speciﬁc quality assurance
(PSQA) outcomes using statistical or artiﬁcial intelli-
gence methods.7–13 Indeed, establishing a robust set of
indicators would beneﬁt the radiotherapy workﬂow by
signiﬁcantly reducing the time and resources devoted
to PSQA measurements, which is one of the main rea-
sons for the increasing interest in this ﬁeld in recent
years. Nonetheless, a lack of agreement over the rel-
evant complexity indicators and a dearth of common
computational tools for metrics extraction are currently
hindering the widespread use of complexity metrics. 14
This issue was highlighted in a recent ESTRO survey
in which only 25% of the participating centers reported
using complexity metrics for PSQA purposes. 15–17
This work introduces a novel free software pack-
age named UCoMX (Universal Complexity Metrics
Extractor), designed to extract complexity metrics from
DICOM-RT plan ﬁles of radiotherapy treatment plans.
The tool is platform-agnostic and has been developed
to seamlessly read DICOM-RT plan ﬁles generated
using various commercially available T reatment Plan-
ning Systems (TPS) across different treatment units.
It is highly versatile and capable of analyzing both
IMRT/VMAT and HT plans. The ultimate goal of this
endeavor is to provide the scientiﬁc community with
a shared tool for extracting complexity metrics, thus
promoting the widespread utilization of these indicators
globally . By freely sharing this tool, we aim to facilitate
the identiﬁcation of reliable and robust strategies for
incorporating complexity evaluation into clinical routine
in the near future.
2 METHODS
The Universal Complexity Metrics Extractor (UCoMX) is
a free software package that extracts complexity met-
rics from radiotherapy treatment plans’ DICOM-RT plan
ﬁles. It was developed using Matlab R2020a (The Math-
Works Inc, Natick, Massachusetts, USA). The UCoMX
package was built to read DICOM-RT plan ﬁles created
for various treatment units using several commercially
available TPSs, including all those available at our
institution and others provided by national and interna-
tional centers, including a modern MRI Linac. A detailed
description of the dataset used to verify the package’s
compatibility is provided in Section 2.3.
UCoMX comprises two distinct extraction engines:
VCoMX (VMAT Complexity Metrics Extractor), designed
for IMRT and VMAT plans, and TCoMX (T omother-
apy Complexity Metrics Extractor), tailored for Helical
T omotherapy plans.18–20 Detailed descriptions of these
engines are provided in Sections 2.1 and 2.2. Presently ,
UCoMX is available in two different versions:
1. The Matlab-based version of UCoMX requires a valid
installation of Matlab. In this version, the code has
been obscured using the p-code format;
2. The stand-alone version of UCoMX works without
the need for Matlab, as it is compiled. An appropri-
ate installation ﬁle is determined by the version of
the operating system and can be obtained from the
authors by request.
Both versions are equipped with a Graphical User
Interface (GUI). However , the Matlab-based version’s
GUI might not be compatible with Matlab releases older
than R2020a. Dedicated text ﬁles are provided for the
user to conﬁgure the input to overcome this problem.
UCoMX is available for download 21 and accompa-
nied by a dedicated user manual which offers detailed
instructions on how to execute the software.
2.1 VCoMX
2.1.1 Complexity metrics
The VMAT/IMRT complexity metrics implemented in
the VCoMX extraction engine were primarily selected
from those summarized in two relevant literature
reviews.5,6 In the package, the metrics are subdivided
into categories according to the classiﬁcation pro-
posed by Crowe 22 and subsequently by Chiavassa and
colleagues.5 Some metrics were purposely excluded
from the current version of UCoMX due to their strong
similarity with other ones. However , for sake of complete-
ness, they will be included in a future version of the
package. For further information on the complexity met-
rics available in the VCoMX engine, please refer to the
UCoMX User Manual and to T able S1 of Supplemen-
tary Material. The following section explains the general
approach adopted for metrics computation.
2.1.2 Control points, control arcs, and
segments: VMAT versus IMRT
The DICOM-RT plan ﬁles for VMAT treatments delin-
eate each delivery arc into several sectors known as
control arcs (CAs), each of which comprises extremals
referred to as control points (CPs). These ﬁles report
the positions of the MLC leaves and jaws, monitor units,
and other relevant information for each CP. Quantities for
each CA are obtained by interpolating the information

2.1.3 Active control arcs and active leaf
pairs
During VMAT treatment delivery , certain portions of arcs
may experience interruptions in radiation. T o account for
this, beam-on CAs are deﬁned as those where a non-
zero number of Monitor Units (MUs) are delivered. The
current version of VCoMX exclusively considers active
arcs, ignoring inactive ones.
Moreover , the active leaf pairs are deﬁned at each CA
based on two criteria. First, facing leaf pairs must form
a gap greater than the minimum possible gap set on
the TPS, which is derived directly from the DICOM-RT
plan ﬁle. Since this value is not stored as a sepa-
rate DICOM header value, the minimum gap over all
plan gaps is utilized. Second, active leaf tips must not
be covered by the jaw . In the present implementation,
leaf tips were considered with no curvature. Both cri-
teria must be satisﬁed for a leaf pair to be included
in the computation of the metrics. In case of treat-
ment units with double-stacked staggered MLC, the two
MLC layers are ﬁrst mapped to a single composite MLC.
The resulting effective MLC leaves have a halved leaf
width due to the staggering of leaves between the two
original MLCs. This effective MLC is then treated as
a standard one and used to calculate the complexity
metrics.

2.1.5 Preliminary validation
The validation of VCoMX’s calculation accuracy primar-
ily relied on a comparison with the TPSs available at
our center . In particular , a characteristic of VMAT/IMRT
DICOM-RT plan ﬁles is the absence of temporal data.
In fact, treatment time is managed by the treatment
machine during delivery and its computation requires
consideration of technical characteristics of the treat-
ment unit such as maximum gantry speed, maximum
leaf speed, and maximum dose rate. However , tem-
poral data is indispensable for the computation of
various metrics, including velocities, accelerations, and
modulation indexes. T o mitigate this limitation, VCoMX
integrates a methodology for the calculation of treat-
ment time. The results generated by VCoMX were
compared with those computed by the TPS available at
our center , utilizing the plans within the provided dataset
for comparison. This comparison is part of the prelimi-
nary validation process performed to check the package
accuracy .
2.2 TCoMX
2.2.1 Complexity metrics
The TCoMX extraction engine implements 51 metrics for
Helical T omotherapy plans selected among those pro-
posed in relevant literature. 19,20,23–26 The metrics are
classiﬁed into three categories based on the features
they described, namely , delivery/TPS, leaf open time
(LOT) statistics, and sinogram. For a detailed explana-
tion of the categories and of the corresponding metrics
and their computation, please refer to the UCoMX User
Manual.
2.2.2 Sinogram and plan value
In the DICOM-RT ﬁles of Helical T omotherapy plans,
each complete gantry rotation is divided into 51 projec-
tions. A 2D matrix called a sinogram stores the fractional
LOT values (relative to the projection time) for each
binary leaf at each projection. TCoMX computes com-
plexity metrics using this data. T o obtain the absolute
LOT s, the sinogram is multiplied by the projection time,
which is stored as a distinct ﬁeld in the DICOM-RT plan
ﬁle.

whether it can be calculated for each projection or

3 RESULTS
3.1 Metrics extraction workﬂow
Figure 2 illustrates a schematic of the extraction pro-
cess with UCoMX. The system consists of two layers:
the GUI interface and the back-end libraries. Users
interact exclusively with the GUI interface, while the
management of the second layer is automated by the
package.
T o initiate the usage of UCoMX, the user should ﬁrst
select the desired extraction engine from the main panel.
Subsequently , a new panel will appear , prompting the
user to input the desired input and output folders. The
input folder should contain the DICOM-RT plan ﬁles for
analysis, which can also be organized into subfolders,
while the output folder is used to store the results. Alter-
natively , users can place an xlsx ﬁle in the input folder
containing the path and ﬁle name of the plans to be
analyzed. In the absence of the GUI, all necessary infor-
mation must be provided through a dedicated text ﬁle
called CONFIG.in.
For VMAT plans, certain machine-related parameters
not stored in the DICOM-RT plan ﬁle (e.g., maximum
gantry speed, maximum MLC leaf speed) can be pro-
vided using a special ﬁle. This ﬁle must be stored in a
subfolder speciﬁcally prepared for this purpose within
the UCoMX main folder . These parameters are essential
for the extraction engine to estimate delivery time and
accurately derive all dynamic quantities (e.g., speed or
acceleration). Default parameters are utilized if this ﬁle
is not provided.
The “Select metrics” button allows users to access
a dedicated panel for creating the list of metrics to be
extracted. In the absence of the GUI, users must man-
ually populate a ﬁle called METRICS.in with the desired
list of metrics.
Upon completion of each execution,UCoMX automat-
ically stores the following output ﬁles:
∙ A datasheet (dataset.xlsx) summarizing information
about the analyzed DICOM-RT plan ﬁles and report-
ing values of the extracted complexity metrics. This
ﬁle is also saved in .mat format.
∙ A copy of the input ﬁles used (CONFIG.in and
METRICS.in).
∙ A log ﬁle containing essential details of the execution.
These output ﬁles are automatically stored in a
subfolder within the designated output folder . This sub-
folder’s name is determined by the session’s date and
time to avoid overwriting older ﬁles in the event of
multiple executions.
3.2 Reference plans on the TG-119
phantom
Figure 3 summarizes the results of the metrics extrac-
tion from the 28 VMAT and the 14 IMRT plans created
at our centers on the cases in the TG-119 phantom
and described in Section 2.3. A total of 43 metrics were
extracted. Some metrics—indicated by grey squares in
the heat map—do not apply to certain plans. On the one
hand, the bev-jaws area ratio (BJAR) and jaw area (JA)
do not apply to ETHOS (Varian Medical System, Palo
Alto, California, USA) as this machine lacks jaws. On
the other hand, LNA, MUdeg, L TAL mDRV , mGSV , GS,
AL, CAL, and GT do not apply to IMRT plans as they
describe dynamical variations not occurring in IMRT
plans (e.g. dose rate variation or gantry rotations).
The complexity indicators display different values for
the three groups of plans (VMAT LM, VMAT HM, and
IMRT) due to varying modulation levels. Additionally , the
metric values vary depending on the treatment machine,
TPS, and individual case (see Section 2.3).
Figure 4 shows the same results for the 16 helical
tomotherapy plans in the dataset. A total of 66 indica-
tors were extracted,where some metrics were computed
for multiple values of their free parameters. Similarly , as
evidenced by the varying patterns depicted in the two
maps, different levels of modulation (HT LM vs. HT LM)
result in different values for the complexity metrics of the
same plan.

4 DISCUSSION
This work introduces UCoMX, a free software package
developed to extract complexity metrics from radio-
therapy treatment plans. With its wide set of included
metrics, different available versions—including Matlab-
based and stand-alone—and user-friendly interface,
UCoMX aims to cater the needs of all users. Fur-
thermore, thanks to its compatibility with the majority
of commercially available TPSs and treatment units,
this software package paves the way toward an all-
around assessment of treatment plan complexity across
institutions.
By providing free access, UCoMX is poised to address
prevailing challenges and limitations regarding the
comparison of results between different institutions.

despite their usage in various studies applied to ﬂuence
maps, gamma maps or dose difference maps. 7,28–30
All those features were proposed before 2015, but
the ﬁeld of radiomics has progressed in recent years,
including by deﬁning standardized features and soft-
ware for their computation. 31–33 As a result, UCoMX is
being developed as a modern and standardized tool,
and its integration with S-IBEX is currently in progress.
S-IBEX is an open-source standardized tool for extract-
ing radiomics features according to the IBSI standard.
Finally , new functionalities for visualizing and compar-
ing plans, along with analyzing machine log ﬁles, are
expected in the future.

5 CONCLUSION
This technical note introduced UCoMX, a new software
package designed to extract complexity metrics from
DICOM-RT plan ﬁles of radiotherapy treatment plans.
The tool is platform-agnostic, engineered to seamlessly
operate with DICOM-RT plan ﬁles created using diverse
commercially available TPSs across various treatment
units. UCoMX is freely available at this repository , 21
complemented by a dataset that can be used as refer-
ence for future comparisons. This dataset aims to foster
comparison of complexity indicators across institutions,
with the overarching goal of facilitating the identiﬁ-
cation of dependable and resilient strategies to inte-
grate these indicators into clinical practice in the near
future.
